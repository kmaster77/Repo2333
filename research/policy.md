Agent 1 (Policy & regulation analyst). Status date: 22 October 2026. Prices in 2026 €, excl. VAT.
Labels: **[VERIFIED]** = source read; **[ESTIMATE]** = derived, calculation shown; **[ASSUMPTION]** = no source, reasoning given.

## 0. Summary in ten lines

1. The **Betonakkoord** still exists and is in its "mainstreaming" phase (2026–2030). Its targets were raised: the ambition is now **at least −70% CO₂ in 2030 vs 1990** and **all released concrete back into the chain**. In reality only **<15%** of ~15 Mt concrete rubble went back into new concrete in mid-2025 [VERIFIED, POL-01, POL-02].
2. The strongest **demand driver** is coming in public infrastructure (GWW): a draft decree makes **maximum MKI values for concrete compulsory in all public GWW tenders**, plus an **MKI award criterion above the EU threshold**, intended from **2027** [VERIFIED, POL-17, POL-18]. Levels are not yet set.
3. Tender rewards are real money: ProRail gives about **25% award advantage** (factor 4 × MKI); a typical fictive discount is **€1–5 per € MKI saved** [VERIFIED, POL-23, POL-24]. For concrete, the gap between the Betonakkoord ceiling and front-runner values is **~€2.8 MKI per m³** in 2026 [VERIFIED, POL-01] → fictive advantage of roughly **€3–15 per m³** [ESTIMATE].
4. **Buildings (MPG) give little pull.** The planned tightening of the homes limit (0.8 → 0.5) was **cancelled in June 2025**. From 1 July 2026 a new method (A2) applies; homes 1.6 (same strictness), offices 1.55 (−15%) [VERIFIED, POL-08, POL-10]. The system stays until at least 2030 [VERIFIED, POL-11].
5. **EU ETS + CBAM** make clinker more expensive. Under current law free allocation for cement falls from 97.5% (2026) to 0% (2034) [VERIFIED, POL-39]. Base estimate: carbon cost in CEM I rises from ~€14/t (2026) to ~€45/t (2030) and ~€95/t (2034) [ESTIMATE]. A **July 2026 Commission proposal would slow this to 2038** [VERIFIED, POL-42] — a downside risk for low-clinker products.
6. **Norms allow only modest recycled coarse aggregate** without extra design work: NEN 8005 allows **30%** (most exposure classes) or **50%** (X0) of the coarse fraction (V/V) for Type A1 granulate [VERIFIED, POL-30]. CUR 112 and CROW-CUR 127 allow more (up to 100% coarse; fines under conditions).
7. **Waste policy does not push concrete-to-concrete.** LAP3 was replaced by the **Circulair Materialenplan (CMP)** on 30 Dec 2025. Its minimum standard for concrete waste is simply "recycling" — road base counts. >90% of rubble still goes to road base [VERIFIED, POL-54, POL-55].
8. **Landfill tax** is €40.85/t in 2026; the 2027 tax plan proposes €64 (2028) → €81/t (2030+) [VERIFIED, POL-50, POL-51]. Stony waste may not be landfilled anyway, so the effect on this business is small.
9. **No levy on primary sand/gravel** is proposed. The **national CO₂ levy for industry** is effectively zero and the cabinet wants to abolish it [VERIFIED, POL-57, POL-58].
10. **Subsidies are modest**: MIA 27–45% deduction (net ~7–12% of eligible capex), Vamil 75%, WBSO 36%/50%, DEI+ (circular economy budget €60 m in 2026), VEKI (€123 m) [VERIFIED, POL-68–POL-76]. They help, but do not make or break the case.

## 1. Betonakkoord (Concrete Agreement, 2018)

**What it is.** A voluntary national chain agreement signed 10 July 2018 by about 30–50 parties: four ministries, Rijkswaterstaat (RWS), ProRail, contractors (BAM, Strukton, VolkerWessels), producers (Cementbouw, VBI) [VERIFIED, POL-03, POL-07].

**Original targets (2018).** In 2030: at least **30% CO₂ reduction vs 1990** and **100% high-grade reuse of released concrete** [VERIFIED, POL-03]. A wider **49% ambition** for 2030 was also communicated [VERIFIED, POL-07, POL-01].

**Anchor check.** The anchor "−30% by 2021" is **wrong**. The 30% was a 2030 target. In 2021 the ambitions were raised: −10% in 2023 vs 2021, "substantially more" in 2025, and **≥70% CO₂ reduction in 2030 vs 1990** (if possible climate neutral) [VERIFIED, POL-04, POL-05].

**Current status (2025–2026).**
- Phases: preparation (2016–18), build-up (2018–21), scale-up (2021–26), **mainstreaming 2026–2030** [VERIFIED, POL-02].
- An independent expert committee published **"De koers naar 2030"** (June 2025) with **ceiling values** (plafondwaarden = minimum level for everyone) and **front-runner values** (koploperwaarden = best 2–3 years ahead) for 2025, 2026, 2027 [VERIFIED, POL-01]. The committee will move to **CROW / IenW** [VERIFIED, POL-02].
- Rule for tightening: the MKI for the "peloton" is tightened by **at least 7.5% per year** on average [VERIFIED, POL-02, POL-05].
- In 2017 the sector was already 24% below 1990. Ceiling values market-wide would reach the old 49% target by 2027; front-runner values market-wide are needed for 70% [VERIFIED, POL-01].
- **No published measured CO₂ result for 2024/2025** was found. This is a data gap.

**Ceiling and front-runner values (concrete only, LCA modules A1–A3, set A1)** [VERIFIED, POL-01]:

| Exposure category | MKI ceiling 2025 / 2026 / 2027 (€/m³) | MKI front-runner 2025 / 2026 / 2027 (€/m³) | CO₂ ceiling 2025 / 2026 / 2027 (kg/m³) | CO₂ front-runner 2025 / 2026 / 2027 (kg/m³) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unreinforced | 10.2 / 9.5 / 8.8 | 7.4 / 7.1 / 6.7 | 157 / 145 / 133 | 109 / 103 / 97 |
| Inside | 10.8 / 10.1 / 9.4 | 7.9 / 7.5 / 7.2 | 169 / 156 / 143 | 117 / 111 / 104 |
| Outside | 11.7 / 10.9 / 10.1 | 8.4 / 8.0 / 7.6 | 183 / 169 / 155 | 126 / 119 / 112 |
| Aggressive | 12.8 / 11.9 / 11.0 | 9.2 / 8.8 / 8.3 | 202 / 186 / 171 | 139 / 131 / 123 |

Note: the ceiling values imply that almost all CEM I must be replaced by CEM III/A; front-runner values assume CEM III/B [VERIFIED, POL-01]. So the CO₂ part is mainly a **binder** question, not an aggregate question.

**Recycling targets (share of recycled concrete in total aggregate, sand + gravel)** [VERIFIED, POL-01, POL-02]:

| Category | Target value 2025–2027 | "Limit value" 2025–2027 |
|---|---|---|
| Unreinforced | 25% | 35% |
| Inside | 15% | 25% |
| Outside | 5% | 15% |
| Aggressive | 0% | 5% |

National path for released concrete rubble going back into concrete: **<15% (2025) → 20% (2026) → 30% (2027) → 40% (2028) → 50% (2029) → >60% (2030)** [VERIFIED, POL-01]. The Nov 2025 update says the indicative circularity requirements are "**not yet introduced**" [VERIFIED, POL-02].

**Clients.** In July 2023 six large infrastructure owners decided to apply the minimum requirements as standard [VERIFIED, POL-20]. For housing/utility (B&U), private clients dominate. The Betonakkoord wants to bind **70% of them (by turnover)** in a voluntary convenant [VERIFIED, POL-02]. On 11 June 2026, **21 developers and builders** signed an intent declaration: from 2026 part of their projects on front-runner values, the rest on ceiling values [VERIFIED, POL-06].

**Effect on the business.** (+) A predictable, published path of requirements is a ready-made product spec for a low-CO₂, recycled-aggregate concrete. (+) Front-runner values create a premium segment. (−) It is voluntary for private clients. (−) The recycling side is weak: targets are low for reinforced outdoor concrete (5–15%) and not yet enforced.
**Certainty:** existence and values High; enforcement in private B&U Low–Medium.

## 2. MPG (Milieuprestatie Gebouwen) and EU whole-life carbon

**What it is.** A legal limit on the embodied environmental impact of new buildings, in € "shadow cost" per m² gross floor area per year. It is required for the building permit (Bbl art. 4.157–4.159) [VERIFIED, POL-08]. The MPG already uses the same shadow-cost logic as the MKI; there is no switch "from MPG to MKI".

**Values.**
- Until 30 June 2026: **0.8** for homes (since 1 July 2021) and **1.0** for offices >100 m² [VERIFIED, POL-08, POL-13]. Anchor confirmed.
- From **1 July 2026**: new **Bepalingsmethode 2.0** and mandatory dataset **A2** (EN 15804+A2, 19 instead of 11 impact categories) [VERIFIED, POL-09]. Numbers go up because more categories count:
  - homes **1.6** (a recalculation, *not* stricter),
  - offices **1.55** (−15% in real terms),
  - retail, schools, healthcare, assembly etc. mostly **1.85**; other functions 1.8 (first time),
  - softer limits for small homes (<80 m² ground-bound, <60 m² flats) and non-compact offices [VERIFIED, POL-08, POL-09, POL-11].

**Anchor correction.** The tightening of homes to **0.5 (old method) was NOT decided**. It was planned for 2025, delayed, then dropped on **23 June 2025** after the STOER advice ("cut contradictory and unnecessary rules") [VERIFIED, POL-10, POL-12]. The minister said the system stays **at least until 2030** and BZK will evaluate the MPG [VERIFIED, POL-11]. EIB found that 0.8 "is usually easily met" [VERIFIED, POL-13]. So for homes, the MPG gives almost no pull for low-CO₂ concrete.

**EU EPBD recast (Directive 2024/1275).**
- Life-cycle **GWP must be calculated and shown on the energy label**: from **1 Jan 2028** for new buildings >1,000 m², from **1 Jan 2030** for all new buildings [VERIFIED, POL-14, POL-15].
- Member states publish a **roadmap for limit values by 1 Jan 2027**, aiming at limit values for new buildings **from 2030** [VERIFIED, POL-14].
- The EU calculation framework (Delegated Reg. 2026/52) was published on 4 May 2026 [VERIFIED, POL-16].
- **Not a cap yet.** Only disclosure is legally fixed. Aedes reports that Dutch WLC rules follow "at the earliest from 2030" [VERIFIED, POL-11]; the Dutch transposition status was not verified.

**Effect on the business.** (+) Disclosure from 2028 makes embodied carbon visible to developers and investors; low-CO₂ concrete becomes a selling point. (+) Offices −15% from mid-2026 gives a small pull. (−) No binding pull in housing before ~2030–2032.
**Certainty:** values from 1 July 2026 High; no homes tightening before 2030 High; Dutch GWP limit values from 2030 Low (A-POL-16).

**Related MIA incentive.** For MIA "circular building" codes, the 2025 handbook uses stricter MPG thresholds (A2), e.g. €0.60 for large ground-bound homes, €0.90 for utility buildings [VERIFIED, POL-95]. This rewards clients, not the producer directly.

## 3. MKI / DuboCalc in public tenders (GWW)

**What MKI is.** The **Milieukostenindicator** expresses all LCA impacts of a material or project in euros of shadow cost. **DuboCalc** is RWS's tool to calculate it from the Nationale Milieudatabase [VERIFIED, POL-28].

**How the reward works (EMVI / BPKV = best price-quality ratio)** [VERIFIED, POL-21, POL-22]:
- **MKI-valuation (MKI-W):** fictive bid = price + MKI × factor. Lower MKI → lower fictive price.
- **Fictive discount:** a € discount on the bid price, usually linear between a reference MKI (no discount) and a target MKI (maximum discount).
- **Quality points:** MKI converted into points.
The lowest fictive price wins. The contractor still gets paid the real bid price.

**Typical size of the reward.**

| Client / source | Mechanism | Size | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| RWS example (2020 webinar) | Max fictive discount €4.0 m over MKI range 570k–1,350k | **€5.13 per € MKI** | [VERIFIED, POL-23] |
| RWS plan (2020) | MKW = MKI × factor | **factor €2 per € MKI** (average of past tenders) | [VERIFIED, POL-23] |
| ProRail (July 2025) | Award advantage via MKI, scope A1–A5 | **±25% advantage (factor 4 × reference MKI)** | [VERIFIED, POL-24] |
| ProRail tender (TED 361891-2025) | MKI linear between reference and target; CO₂-Prestatieladder discount | Ladder level 1–5 = **1%–10%** of bid | [VERIFIED, POL-25] |
| PIANOo guidance | MKI-W examples | factor 1, 4 or 10 | [VERIFIED, POL-22] |

The evaluation of MKI purchasing (2023) found that **the valuation varies a lot between clients**, so producers lack investment certainty [VERIFIED, POL-29].

**What this means per m³ of concrete [ESTIMATE].**
Gap between ceiling and front-runner MKI in 2026 = 2.4–3.1 €/m³ (e.g. "outside" 10.9 − 8.0 = 2.9) [POL-01].
Fictive advantage = gap × factor = 2.8 × 3 (base) = **€8.4/m³**; range 2.4 × 1 = €2.4 to 3.1 × 5 = €15.5/m³ (A-POL-10, A-POL-11).
The advantage goes to the contractor's bid. If the concrete supplier captures ~40% (A-POL-12, [ASSUMPTION]), the supplier premium is about **€3.4/m³** (range ~€0.5–9/m³). This applies only to tenders with an MKI criterion.
Caveat: most of the MKI gap comes from the binder (CEM III/B, low-clinker). Recycled coarse aggregate lowers MKI only a little (less gravel extraction and transport) [ASSUMPTION — Agent 3 to quantify]. Rijkswaterstaat itself warns that circularity and CO₂ reduction can conflict [VERIFIED, POL-04].

**Coming law: Besluit milieuprestatie-eisen GWW** [VERIFIED, POL-17, POL-18, POL-19]:
- All public clients (state, provinces, municipalities, water boards, special-sector companies) must set **maximum MKI values** for defined forms and mixes of **concrete and asphalt** in **every** GWW tender.
- For tenders above the **EU works threshold**, the **MKI of the whole work becomes a compulsory BPKV criterion**.
- Internet consultation 6 July–24 August 2026 (closed). Intended start **2027**. The levels follow later in a ministerial regulation; CROW works out ceiling values and a RAW paragraph. Steel may follow.
- Our base assumption: start **1 Jan 2028** (range mid-2027 to 2029) (A-POL-13).
- Why it matters: municipalities are **30%** of GWW turnover, grid operators 18%, RWS 11%, ProRail 9%; about half the market (mid/small municipalities, regional grid operators, water boards) rarely uses MKI today [VERIFIED, POL-20]. GWW market ≈ **€22 bn/yr** [VERIFIED, POL-22].

**National MKI reduction ambitions for concrete in GWW.**
- Betonakkoord minimum requirements: **betonmortel (ready-mix) −18.4% MKI/m³** and **concrete paving −23.6% MKI/m³** in 2030 vs 2021 [VERIFIED, POL-20]. The newer "Koers naar 2030" values are stricter (≥7.5%/yr) [VERIFIED, POL-01, POL-02].
- ProRail: focus target **−30% MKI in 2025, −50% in 2030** [VERIFIED, POL-24].

**Rijkswaterstaat "Naar klimaatneutrale en circulaire rijksinfraprojecten".**
- The 2020 strategy aimed at **climate-neutral and circular infra projects in 2030**, with **halving of primary raw materials** [VERIFIED, POL-27].
- **Corrected/updated:** RWS/IenW now state **climate neutral and circular in 2050**, with an interim target of **−30% CO₂ in 2030 vs 2024** [VERIFIED, POL-26]. It uses a "peloton" (standard) + "front-runner" (reward) approach [VERIFIED, POL-26].

**Effect on the business.** (+) Strongest and most bankable demand signal: from ~2028 all public GWW concrete must meet a ceiling, and large works reward lower MKI. (+) Infra concrete is a natural first market for ready-mix/prefab with a verified LCA. (−) Ceiling values will be set at "peloton" level that incumbents can meet; the premium is only in the front-runner gap. (−) Each product needs an LCA/EPD in the NMD (cost, time).
**Certainty:** law in some form Medium–High; exact date and levels Medium; size of reward per tender Medium.

Notes:
- **Fines/sand:** the norm route for recycled sand in structural concrete is very limited; CUR 127 opens it under conditions. Certification bodies' acceptance of CUR 127 was still an open point in 2025 (reader comment, POL-35 — weak evidence).

- BAM and RWS found that most Dutch concrete granulate meets **Type A1** [VERIFIED, POL-35].

**Effect on the business.** (+) 20–30% coarse replacement is allowed without discussion; 50% with CUR 112. (−) Above that, clients and certifiers must accept CUR 112/127 project by project. (−) Recycled sand/fines (half of the rubble mass) have no easy route. Base for the model: **30% of coarse aggregate (V/V)**, range 20–50% (A-POL-25).
**Certainty:** High (norms are in force).

## 5. EU ETS, CBAM and cement cost

**Free allocation phase-out (current law).** EU cement plants receive free allowances = benchmark × activity × **CBAM factor**. The factor falls as CBAM phases in [VERIFIED, POL-39]:

| Year | 2026 | 2027 | 2028 | 2029 | 2030 | 2031 | 2032 | 2033 | 2034 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CBAM factor (share of free allocation kept) | 97.5% | 95% | 90% | 77.5% | 51.5% | 39% | 26.5% | 14% | 0% |
| Equivalent phase-out | 2.5% | 5% | 10% | 22.5% | 48.5% | 61% | 73.5% | 86% | 100% |

- Grey clinker benchmark 2026–2030: **0.656 allowances/t clinker** (Implementing Reg. 2026/1412) [VERIFIED, POL-41].
- **New proposal (17 July 2026, not adopted):** for CBAM sectors, **15% of the phased-out free allocation comes back from 2028**, and the end of free allocation moves **from 2034 to 2038**. The cap declines more slowly (LRF 3.7% 2031–35, 1.7% from 2036) [VERIFIED, POL-42, POL-43]. Trilogue expected from January 2027 [VERIFIED, POL-45].

**CBAM definitive phase.** Since **1 Jan 2026** importers of cement/clinker need to be authorised CBAM declarants and buy certificates. **Omnibus Regulation (EU) 2025/2083** (in force 20 Oct 2025) introduced a **50 t/yr de minimis threshold**, moved certificate sales to **1 Feb 2027** and the first annual declaration to **30 Sep 2027** [VERIFIED, POL-38, POL-39, POL-40, POL-99]. Certificate price 2026: Q1 €75.36, Q2 **€75.28**, Q3 **€82.32** per t CO₂ [VERIFIED, POL-40].

**EUA price.** €84.74/t on 22 Oct 2026 (ICE) [VERIFIED, POL-44]; €88.3 high on 12 Oct [VERIFIED, POL-45]. Forecasts: LSEG (July 2025, before the review) €91 (2027), €114 (2030), €186 (2035) real [VERIFIED, POL-46]; PBL KEV €108 (2030), €141 (2035) [VERIFIED, POL-59]; SEB: avg €75, range €50–100 for five years [VERIFIED, POL-47]. Analysts lowered forecasts after the July proposal [VERIFIED, POL-48]. Our base: **€88 (2027), €100 (2030), €125 (2034)** (A-POL-01–03).
**ETS2** (buildings, road fuel, from 2028) is separate; it affects truck diesel only indirectly [VERIFIED, POL-49].

**Effect on cement cost [ESTIMATE].** Formula: net carbon cost per t cement = (clinker CO₂ intensity − benchmark × CBAM factor) × EUA × clinker share. Inputs: intensity 0.82 t CO₂/t clinker (A-POL-05); clinker share CEM I 0.93, CEM III/A 0.45, CEM III/B 0.25 (A-POL-06).

| Year (current law) | EUA base | Net t CO₂ per t clinker | CEM I (€/t cement) | CEM III/A (€/t) | CEM III/B (€/t) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026 | €85 | 0.82 − 0.656×0.975 = 0.180 | 14 | 7 | 4 |
| 2027 | €88 | 0.197 | 16 | 8 | 4 |
| 2030 | €100 | 0.482 | 45 | 22 | 12 |
| 2034 | €125 | 0.820 | 95 | 46 | 26 |

Per m³ of concrete (300 kg CEM I or CEM III/A; 320 kg CEM III/B): 2030 ≈ €13.5 / €6.5 / €3.9 per m³; 2034 ≈ €28.5 / €13.8 / €8.3 per m³ [ESTIMATE].
So the carbon-cost **advantage of a CEM III/B mix vs a CEM III/A mix** grows from ~€1/m³ (2026) to ~€2.6/m³ (2030) and ~€5.5/m³ (2034); vs CEM I from ~€3 to ~€10 and ~€20/m³ [ESTIMATE].
If the 2038 proposal is adopted, 2034 cost is ~10–40% lower (A-POL-07 low case).
Caveats: (1) pass-through to the cement price is assumed 85% (A-POL-09, [ASSUMPTION]); if producers already price the full carbon opportunity cost today, prices rise less. (2) Most Dutch ready-mix already uses CEM III, so the market-wide price shock is smaller than for CEM I. (3) Less blast-furnace slag in future (Tata Steel plans to replace blast furnace 7 with DRI+EAF; timeline slipping, JLoI extended to 1 March 2027) could raise CEM III prices [VERIFIED, POL-93, POL-94, POL-100; effect is ASSUMPTION A-POL-28].

**Certainty:** current-law schedule High; whether the 2038 proposal passes Medium; EUA path Medium–Low.

## 6. Landfill tax, waste plan minimum standard, levies

**Afvalstoffenbelasting (landfill and incineration tax).**
- 2026: **€40.85/t** (2025: €39.70) [VERIFIED, POL-50].
- Belastingplan 2027 (proposal, Sept 2026): **€40.85 (2027), €64 (2028), €71 (2029), €81 from 2030** (2026 prices). This replaces the BP2026 path that went to €92.82 (2028) and €124.20 (2030) [VERIFIED, POL-51, POL-52, POL-53]. Parliament votes before year-end.
- Stony C&D waste (incl. concrete) has a **landfill ban** under the Bssa [VERIFIED, POL-56]. So the tax mostly hits sorting residues, not clean concrete rubble.

**LAP3 → Circulair Materialenplan (CMP).** **Anchor correction:** LAP3 ended on 29 Dec 2025. Since **30 Dec 2025** the **CMP** applies [VERIFIED, POL-54]. The CMP has a dedicated "Beton" chain plan [VERIFIED, POL-55]:
- **Minimum standard for concrete waste = recycling.** All forms count: road base (mixed granulate), concrete granulate in new concrete, or advanced separation into sand/gravel/cement paste. **No preference** for concrete-to-concrete, and no "recycling standard" was set.
- Today **>90%** of concrete rubble goes into mixed granulate for road base; **5–10%** goes into new concrete (2024).
- Concrete waste has no separate waste category yet; keeping concrete separate from masonry at demolition is "desirable" but **not mandatory**.
- Dutch concrete demand in 2030 estimated at **33 Mt**, needing 12.5 Mt sand, 15.3 Mt gravel, 4.8 Mt cement.
- The CMP team works on "further developing the minimum standard" and a draft **Kaderwet circulaire economie** is in consultation (2026) [VERIFIED, POL-97]. A future higher standard is possible but not announced.

**Levy on primary raw materials.** **No** proposal for a levy on primary sand, gravel or construction materials was found in current government documents (searches in this session). [ASSUMPTION: none before 2030; Low probability.]

**National CO₂ levy for industry (CO₂-heffing industrie).**
- Rate cut to **€78.67/t** for 2026–2030; because it is reduced by the ETS price and dispensation rights, it is **effectively zero from 2027** [VERIFIED, POL-58].
- The current coalition intends to **abolish** it; the levy for waste incinerators stays [VERIFIED, POL-57, POL-98].
- For cement: there is no Dutch clinker kiln today [ASSUMPTION — widely known, not re-checked], so the levy has no effect on cement prices. A ready-mix/prefab plant is not covered.

**Effect on the business.** (−) No policy forces rubble out of road base into concrete; recycled aggregate stays a voluntary, price-driven choice. (+/0) Landfill tax rise is mild and indirect. (0) No raw-material levy, no national CO₂ levy.
**Certainty:** High for 2026–2027 values; Medium for the 2028–2030 landfill tax path.

## 7. Permits under the Omgevingswet

**Ready-mix or prefab plant.**
- It is a **milieubelastende activiteit** (environmentally harmful activity) under **Bal §3.4.5 (mineral products industry)**; content rules in **Bal §4.8 "betoncentrale"** (dust from storage and transfer of sand, gravel, cement; wash water). Discharging wash water to surface water needs a **notification 4 weeks ahead** [VERIFIED, POL-60].
- Before 2024 (Bor), a full permit was needed for ready-mix ≥ **100 t/h** and for prefab with presses/vibrating tables ≥ **100 t/day**; smaller plants only notified [VERIFIED, POL-61]. A plant of 100,000 m³/yr (~240,000 t/yr) peaks above 100 t/h, so a permit is likely [ESTIMATE/ASSUMPTION; exact Bal threshold not verified].
- Also needed: building permit, fit with the municipal **omgevingsplan** (zoning; industrial site category for noise/dust), and possibly a **Natura 2000 permit** for nitrogen.

**Crushing/recycling plant (option A: own crushing).**
- Processing of business waste is a permit-requiring activity (**Bal §3.5.11**, with exceptions); a stationary puin-recycling plant needs an **omgevingsvergunning milieubelastende activiteit** [VERIFIED for the principle, POL-62]. The permit is checked against the **CMP minimum standard** [VERIFIED, POL-55].
- **Mobile crushing** at a demolition site only needs a **notification** (example Hoorn, May 2026) [VERIFIED, POL-63].
- Main issues: **noise** (crusher, screens, loaders), **dust** (PM10), traffic, and nitrogen (diesel equipment and trucks).

**Nitrogen (stikstof) / AERIUS.**
- Any extra nitrogen deposition above **0.005 mol/ha/yr** on a Natura 2000 area needs a permit [VERIFIED, POL-65]. Internal netting (intern salderen) is only allowed via an appropriate assessment, i.e. with a permit [VERIFIED, POL-66].
- The cabinet will introduce a **calculation floor ("rekenkundige ondergrens") at the latest by end 2027**; until then AERIUS and the critical deposition values apply [VERIFIED, POL-64]. AERIUS Calculator 2026 was updated on 6 Oct 2026 [VERIFIED, POL-67].
- Electric loaders and trucks, and a site far from Natura 2000 areas, reduce this risk.

**Typical timeline [ASSUMPTION].** Regular procedure 8 weeks (+6 weeks extension); extended procedure about 26 weeks; preparation (noise, dust, AERIUS studies) 3–6 months; objections and appeal can add 6–18 months. Base: **12 months** for a concrete plant on an existing industrial site (range 6–24) and **18 months** for a crushing plant (range 9–36) (A-POL-19, A-POL-20).

**Effect on the business.** (−) Permits add 1–2 years before first revenue and favour sites with existing permits (brownfield, port areas). (+) Buying recycled aggregate (base case B) avoids the harder crushing permit.
**Certainty:** procedure Medium–High; durations Medium–Low.

## 9. EU construction products, end-of-waste, Dutch circular programme

**Construction Products Regulation (EU) 2024/3110.**
- In force 7 Jan 2025; main part applies from **8 Jan 2026**; penalties for environmental declarations from **8 Jan 2027**; CPR-2011 fully repealed around 2039/2040 [VERIFIED, POL-80, POL-83].
- Manufacturers must declare **LCA-based environmental characteristics (incl. GWP)** in the declaration of performance — but only once the **new harmonised standard for their product family** is cited [VERIFIED, POL-80].
- Product families include **precast concrete (family 1)**, **aggregates (24)** and **products related to concrete, mortar and grout (26)** [VERIFIED, POL-79].
- **Cement is first**: standardisation request M/615 (July 2025); CEN enquiry closed July 2026; first mandatory standard "probably 2027" [VERIFIED, POL-81, POL-82].
- Process length per family: ~18–22 months acquis + 6 months request + 24–36 months CEN + ~5 months + 12 months voluntary use [VERIFIED, POL-82]. **[ESTIMATE]:** mandatory GWP declarations for precast concrete and aggregates **not before ~2029–2031**.
- A Digital Product Passport is foreseen [VERIFIED, POL-80].

**End-of-waste (EoW) for C&D waste / aggregates.**
- **No EU-wide EoW criteria** for recycled aggregates exist yet. A 2024 Commission study found aggregates and concrete have the highest potential [VERIFIED, POL-84].
- The **EU Circular Economy Act** should harmonise EoW criteria and boost demand (recycled content, green procurement) [VERIFIED, POL-86]. Its proposal was postponed again, now planned for **18 Nov 2026** [VERIFIED, POL-87].
- The Netherlands already has a **national EoW regulation for recycled granulate** (with conformity declaration) [VERIFIED, POL-85].

**Nationaal Programma Circulaire Economie (NPCE) 2023–2030.**
- Overall: **50% less primary abiotic raw materials in 2030**, fully circular 2050 [VERIFIED, POL-88].
- Construction product groups: homes, offices/halls, **concrete viaducts and bridges**, road paving [VERIFIED, POL-88]. For bridges/viaducts: by 2030 a reuse scan for every structure; of material not reused, **at least 80% high-grade recycled**; all concrete bridges/viaducts to be demolished are circularly demolished [VERIFIED, POL-55].
- PBL (ICER 2025): most NPCE targets are still **qualitative, expired and not met**; the 50% target gives firms too little direction [VERIFIED, POL-89].

**Effect on the business.** (+) CPR/EPD obligations and EPBD disclosure will make low-carbon claims comparable and enforceable around 2029–2031 — good for a front-runner. (0/−) The NPCE is ambition without hard instruments for concrete, apart from the GWW decree.
**Certainty:** CPR timeline Medium; EU EoW Low; NPCE effect Low.

## 11. Implications for the business model

- **Lead with infrastructure (GWW), not housing.** The GWW decree (base 2028) and RWS/ProRail MKI rewards are the only hard demand drivers. Housing MPG gives no real pull before ~2030. A first plant should be close to large public infra clients and contractors.
- **Sell low MKI/CO₂ first, recycled content second.** The tender reward and the coming ceilings are expressed in MKI/CO₂, which is driven mostly by the binder. Recycled aggregate alone earns little in MKI. The product must reach Betonakkoord **front-runner** values (e.g. ≤€7.5–8.0 MKI/m³, ≤111–119 kg CO₂/m³ for inside/outside in 2026) to earn a premium; ceiling values will become the market floor.
- **Size of the green premium is small and tender-bound:** about €3–15/m³ fictive advantage for the contractor, of which perhaps €1–9/m³ reaches the supplier (base ~€3/m³). Do not model a large, general green premium.
- **Carbon cost slowly turns into a structural edge.** Low-clinker mixes save ~€1/m³ (2026) → ~€2.6/m³ (2030) → ~€5.5/m³ (2034) vs CEM III/A, and much more vs CEM I. Model a scenario where the EU slows the phase-out to 2038.
- **Design to the norms that exist:** 30% (V/V) recycled coarse aggregate as standard, 50% where CUR 112 is accepted (X0, unreinforced, inside). Treat >50% and recycled sand as an R&D option (CUR 127, WBSO/DEI+).
- **Do not count on waste policy for cheap supply.** The CMP lets rubble go to road base; there is no landfill route and no raw-material levy. Recycled aggregate competes on price with road base outlets, so secure supply contracts with demolition firms (or own crushing as option A).
- **Plan 12–24 months for permits** and pick a site with existing industrial zoning, far from Natura 2000, with electric equipment. This argues for buying aggregate in the base case.
- **Subsidies are a bonus, not a pillar.** Include MIA/Vamil (~9% of qualifying capex) and WBSO; treat DEI+/VEKI as upside only. Budget for LCA/EPD registration in the NMD for each mix — required to compete in MKI tenders now and under the CPR later.

## 12. Domain anchors: confirmed vs corrected

**Confirmed**
- MPG 0.8 for homes since 1 July 2021; 1.0 for offices (>100 m²) [POL-08, POL-13].
- EPBD: GWP disclosure from 2028 (>1,000 m²) and 2030 (all new buildings) [POL-14, POL-15].
- Betonakkoord target of 100% high-grade reuse of released concrete by 2030 (as ambition) [POL-03, POL-04].
- EU ETS free allocation phase-out for cement 2026–2034 under CBAM (current law) [POL-39].
- CBAM definitive phase from 1 Jan 2026, cement covered; Omnibus simplification adopted (50 t threshold, certificates from Feb 2027) [POL-38, POL-39].
- NEN-EN 206 + NEN 8005 limits of 50%/30%/20% for coarse recycled aggregate; CUR 112 up to 100% under conditions [POL-30, POL-31].
- BRL 2506 parts 1 (KOMO technical) and 2 (NL-BSB environmental) [POL-33].
- Betonakkoord national MKI reduction for ready-mix in GWW (−18.4% MKI/m³ 2030 vs 2021) [POL-20].

**Corrected or updated**
- **Betonakkoord "−30% by 2021"** → wrong. 2018: ≥30% by **2030**; ambition raised in 2021 to **≥70% by 2030**; the 49% figure was the earlier ambition [POL-01, POL-03, POL-04].
- **MPG tightening to ~0.5 by 2030** → **cancelled** (June 2025). From 1 July 2026 new A2 method: homes 1.6 (policy-neutral), offices 1.55 (−15%); system kept until at least 2030 [POL-08, POL-10, POL-11].
- **"MPG to MKI-based rules"** → the MPG already uses shadow costs; the change is set A1 → **A2 (EN 15804+A2)** and Bepalingsmethode 2.0 [POL-09].
- **EPBD "cap on embodied carbon"** → only disclosure is fixed; limit values are a roadmap item for ~2030, not yet law [POL-14].
- **ETS phase-out ends in 2034** → still law, but a **July 2026 proposal would extend it to 2038** with 15% of allocation restored from 2028 [POL-42, POL-43].
- **LAP3 minimum standard** → LAP3 **expired 29 Dec 2025**; replaced by the **Circulair Materialenplan**; minimum standard for concrete = recycling in any form [POL-54, POL-55].
- **RWS "climate neutral and circular by 2030"** → now **2050**, with **−30% CO₂ by 2030 vs 2024** [POL-26, POL-27].
- **Dutch CO₂ levy for industry** → effectively zero since 2026–2027 and set to be abolished [POL-57, POL-58].
- **New**: mandatory MKI ceilings for concrete in all public GWW tenders (draft decree, intended 2027) — not in the original anchors [POL-17].
