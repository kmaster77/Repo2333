# Market & demand — circular ready-mix / prefab concrete, Netherlands

Agent 2 (Market & demand analyst). Date: 2026-10-22. Prices in 2026 €, excluding VAT, unless stated.
Sources: `research/sources_market.md` (MKT-xx). Assumptions: `research/assumptions_market.md` (A-MKT-xx).
Labels: **[VERIFIED]** = source found and read; **[ESTIMATE]** = calculated from sources (calculation shown); **[ASSUMPTION]** = no source, reasoning given.

Terms used once and explained:
- **Ready-mix (betonmortel)**: fresh concrete mixed at a plant and brought to site in a truck mixer.
- **Prefab / concrete products (betonproducten)**: hardened concrete made in a factory (floors, walls, piles, pavers, pipes).
- **Betongranulaat**: crushed concrete (≥80% concrete) used as gravel replacement. **Menggranulaat**: crushed mix of concrete and brick, used under roads.
- **GWW**: civil works (roads, bridges, water). **B&U**: buildings (housing and utility).
- **MKI** (milieukostenindicator): the environmental cost of a product in euros, used in Dutch tenders. **MPG**: legal environmental limit for new buildings.
- **Gate fee**: what a crusher charges to accept demolition concrete.

## Summary (the 8 things an investor should know)

1. **The market is ~14 Mm³ concrete per year** (~33 Mt). Ready-mix ~7.4 Mm³ (53%), products/prefab ~5.3 Mm³ (38%). Cement use is 3.5–4 Mt/yr. Volume is flat over the long run. [VERIFIED, MKT-01, MKT-03]
2. **Concrete rubble is plentiful: ~11.5–15 Mt/yr** (≈60% of 19–25 Mt stony demolition waste). Only **~1.0–2.0 Mt (8–15%) goes back into concrete** today. The rest goes mainly into road base. Landfill is near zero. [VERIFIED, MKT-05, MKT-06, MKT-07, MKT-09]
3. **The road-base outlet will shrink by rule.** The Betonakkoord caps concrete in menggranulaat at 30% (2025), 15% (2027), 5% (2030), and targets 20% → >60% of rubble back into concrete in 2026 → 2030. So supply of recycled aggregate will grow strongly. Feedstock is **not** the bottleneck for one plant; quality is. [VERIFIED, MKT-05, MKT-11]
4. **Primary gravel is ~75% imported**; the last large Dutch Maas gravel project ends in late 2026. Government expects no shortage for 10 years. Prices of primary aggregates have tracked general construction costs. [VERIFIED, MKT-15, MKT-16, MKT-17, MKT-18]
5. **Ready-mix sells at ~€110–165/m³ delivered on projects** (C20/25 €110–130; C30/37 €140–165). List prices are ~€200–225/m³. [VERIFIED as published indications, MKT-24, MKT-27]
6. **Recycled content today is sold with a surcharge, not a discount.** The market leader charges +€3.30 (30% granulate) to +€11/m³ (100%) extra; Holcim +€4/m³ and +€5–9/m³ for its low-CO₂ ECOPact line. That is +2–8% of project price. [VERIFIED, MKT-24, MKT-25]
7. **Willingness to pay is weak in cash; tenders reward it in "fictive" euros.** Rijkswaterstaat/ProRail convert lower MKI into a fictive bid discount (factor 2–5 × MKI saved). Evidence of real cash premiums is thin (PBL: recycled concrete ~10% dearer, "much of the sector still buys on lowest price"). Base case: **0–3% cash premium**, mainly in public/MKI-driven work. [VERIFIED, MKT-42, MKT-43, MKT-44, MKT-47; ESTIMATE A-MKT-25]
8. **TAM ≈ €2.7bn/yr; national SAM (circular-addressable) ≈ €0.4bn ready-mix + ~€0.8bn prefab; SOM for one ready-mix plant ≈ 60k m³ ≈ €8–9m revenue/yr.** Best first region: **Zuid-Holland (Rotterdam–Drechtsteden)**, then **Metropoolregio Amsterdam**, then **Noord-Brabant**. [ESTIMATE, §8–9]

### Is road-base demand shrinking? Yes, and by policy, not only by market

- The Betonakkoord circularity rules cap the **concrete share in menggranulaat at 30% (2025), 15% (2027) and 5% (2030)**. Road base must then use more brick-rich granulate or slag mixes. [VERIFIED, MKT-11]
- The Betonakkoord path for rubble back into concrete is **<15% (2025) → 20% (2026) → 30% (2027) → 40% (2028) → 50% (2029) → >60% (2030)**. [VERIFIED, MKT-05]
- Researchers and BRBS speak of a coming "surplus of 15 Mt concrete rubble" next to a shortage of primary gravel. [VERIFIED, MKT-13]
- Few new roads are planned; the infra budget goes to renewal of existing structures (see §6). [VERIFIED, MKT-40, MKT-41]

**What this means for archetype B (buyer of recycled aggregate) [ESTIMATE]:**
- If the 2027 target (30%) is met: 0.30 × 13 Mt ≈ 3.9 Mt/yr must go into concrete, vs ~1.5 Mt now. At ~0.4 t granulate per m³ circular concrete (A-MKT-29) that is ~10 Mm³ — about 70% of all Dutch concrete. The target is very ambitious; it would make recycled content normal, not special.
- Supply of certified betongranulaat should rise and its price stay low (BE lists €9.5–18/t, MKT-20–22). This is good for input cost, but it also means **recycled content will not stay a differentiator** for long.
- Gate fees: clean concrete becomes a wanted feedstock (Belgian crushers already accept clean concrete for €0–1/t, MKT-20–22), while mixed/dirty rubble gets more expensive to place.
- Caution: progress is slow. VolkerWessels reports its granulate use *fell* in 2024 because of limited availability and client requirements. [VERIFIED, MKT-51] Clean, certified supply (BRL 2506-1) is the real constraint, not tonnes.

| Tender mechanisms (NL) | RWS: MKI × €2 added to bid price (planned method); ProRail: award advantage ±25% (factor 4 × MKI reference); Betonakkoord suggests X = 5 for ready-mix; CO₂-Ladder 2–6% fictive discount [MKT-42–45] | Public tenders **reward** lower MKI in fictive euros. ### Worked example: how much is lower MKI worth in a tender? [ESTIMATE, A-MKT-27, A-MKT-28]

- Standard ready-mix MKI ≈ €10–25/m³; 2021 ceiling €16–22/m³ [MKT-42, MKT-46].
- Assume a circular low-clinker mix saves ΔMKI = €5/m³ (range €2–8).
- Fictive value per m³ = factor × ΔMKI = 2 × 5 = **€10** (RWS), 4 × 5 = **€20** (ProRail), 5 × 5 = **€25** (Betonakkoord X-factor).
- Versus a C30/37 price of €145/m³: **7–17% "virtual" advantage**.
- Example viaduct with 5,000 m³: ΔMKI = €25,000 → fictive discount €50k–125k on the bid.

**Critical points:**
1. The fictive discount goes to the **bidder (contractor)**, not to the concrete supplier. The supplier gets a premium only if competing suppliers cannot offer the same MKI.
2. The cheapest MKI levers (CEM III/B, which is already the Dutch default, slag/fly-ash, less cement) are available to **all** incumbents. Recycled aggregate lowers MKI only a little; most of concrete's MKI is in the binder.
3. MKI ceilings fall ≥7.5%/yr [MKT-56]. Today's "green" mix becomes tomorrow's minimum. The premium window per product is 2–3 years ("koploper becomes peloton").
4. In private B&U the MPG pushes low-MKI binders, not recycled aggregate. Cash premium ≈ 0%.

**Conclusion:** there is **good evidence that public tenders reward lower MKI, and weak evidence that customers pay more in cash**. Base case for the model: **cash premium 3% (0–8%) in public/MKI-driven work and 0% (−2% to +3%) in private work** [ASSUMPTION A-MKT-25, A-MKT-26]. The stronger commercial argument is **win rate / volume** in MKI tenders, plus lower input cost once betongranulaat is abundant — not a price premium.

### Ranking and reasons

1. **Zuid-Holland (Rotterdam–Drechtsteden)** — the largest housing pipeline (25% of all woondeal homes, 84% apartments), the heaviest RWS bridge-renewal cluster, the most rubble (~2.7 Mt/yr), and the best waterway access for imported gravel and barge-borne rubble. Risk: strong incumbents (Heidelberg's port plants, Renewi's 150 kt upcycling plant).
2. **Metropoolregio Amsterdam** — the most new homes in 2025 (14.0k), the strongest circular procurement culture (Amsterdam, MRA Circulaire Deal), ~2 Mt/yr demolition concrete. Best region if the plant is sold on *premium circular* positioning. Risk: Rutte Groep already sells circular concrete with own cement recovery; grid congestion in Amsterdam.
3. **Noord-Brabant** — solid demand (157k woondeal homes, +26k extra agreed), close to Belgian/German aggregates and Maas shipping, fewer circular specialists. Lower circular pull and less rubble density.

The choice between 1 and 2 depends on the strategy: **volume and infra (Zuid-Holland) vs premium circular positioning (MRA)**. The financial model should test both via price premium and utilisation.
